# Finance Domain Tenets

Condensed from books actually read and indexed in this environment's library (Housel, Sethi, Stanley & Danko, Ramsey, Clason, Tony Robbins, Collins, Jarvis, Martell, Weiss) — not generic training-data platitudes. Use these to pick the right shape of Direct Grant for a wealth/money wish, keyed to the resource-gap axis from `intent-axes.md`.

## Pick the framework by resource-gap read

| Resource-gap read | Framework to reach for | Core mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| In debt, or income feels chaotic/out of control | Ramsey's Baby Steps + Debt Snowball | Pay minimums on everything, attack the *smallest* balance first regardless of interest rate — behaviorally, the fast small wins sustain motivation better than the mathematically optimal highest-interest-first order. Behavior beats math here because the plan that gets abandoned is worth less than the "suboptimal" one that gets finished. |
| Has income, no system, wants to know where money should go | Sethi's Conscious Spending Plan | The "magic wand" exercise: imagine dividing income into a pie (fixed costs, savings, investments, guilt-free spending) — the point isn't precision, it's making the allocation *deliberate* instead of default. Reframe from "how much do I need to make" to "what do I want to do with my life, and how does money serve that." Automate the system once chosen so it doesn't rely on daily willpower. |
| Has income and some savings, wants a simple starting discipline | Clason's "pay yourself first" | Save (classically 10%) off the top before any spending happens, treated as non-negotiable as any other bill — the oldest version of "automate it" and still the right default when someone just needs one rule to start with. |
| Wants to know if they're "on track" independent of income level | Stanley & Danko's seven factors | Wealth correlates with *behavior* (living below means, allocating time/energy/money toward wealth-building, valuing independence over status-display) far more than with income level. Concrete test: never buy a home requiring a mortgage more than 2x household income — a good gut-check number to hand someone regardless of their specific numbers. |
| The wish carries disproportionate urgency, guilt, or anxiety about money | Housel's ego-vs-income framing (pairs with Da Rules category 6) | "Savings is the gap between your ego and your income" — the reliable lever for savings is lowering the ego-driven spending (wanting to visibly *look* successful), not raising income. People with durable financial security tend to not care much what others think of their spending. If a wish's intensity seems driven by comparison/status rather than a concrete goal, this is the one-line frame to hand back, alongside Robbins' observation that many people carry unexamined negative associations with wealth itself (guilt, fear of judgment, "money is dirty") that quietly cap what they let themselves earn or keep — worth naming if the wish reads as self-sabotaging rather than just underplanned. |

## How to combine these in a Direct Grant

Most real wealth wishes need two of these stacked, not one in isolation: a debt-or-income-shaped mechanism (Ramsey or Sethi, whichever fits) *plus* a behavioral check (Stanley/Housel) so the plan doesn't just optimize the wrong thing. Don't dump all five frameworks on someone — pick the one or two that match the resource-gap read and name it, so the plan reads as decisive rather than a book report.

## Investing mechanics (once the "automate it" habit already exists)

| Resource-gap read | Framework to reach for | Core mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Has money accumulating (or a 401(k) sitting untouched) and no idea what to actually invest *in* | Collins' Simple Path to Wealth (one/two-fund portfolio) | A single low-cost total-market index fund (VTSAX or equivalent) captures nearly all of what asset allocation can give you during the accumulation years — skip stock-picking and manager-picking entirely, they're a net drag after fees. Split into stage, not age: while still earning and adding money, lean all/mostly stocks (Wealth Accumulation); once living off the portfolio or within ~5-10 years of that, add a bond fund for stability (Wealth Preservation). The number of years to retirement matters more than the calendar age itself. |
| Wants a real, concrete starting point on retirement accounts — 401(k)/IRA feel like a foreign language | Sethi's tax-advantaged account order of operations | Contribute to the 401(k) only up to the full employer match first (it's an instant, guaranteed ~25-40% return via free money + pretax compounding) → then max a Roth IRA (contributions taxed now, growth and withdrawals tax-free forever — the "pay the tax while the balance is small" trade) → then return to the 401(k) and raise the contribution further. Tax-advantaged wrappers always come before a plain taxable brokerage account, regardless of what's being invested in. |
| Reading market volatility as a reason to go all-in on one asset or freeze entirely | Robbins' needs-based asset allocation (Unshakeable) | Don't hand someone a generic risk-tolerance quiz result — the real design question is "what mix of assets gives the highest odds of getting from where I am to where I need to be," because a risk-averse person who under-invests in stocks can be just as doomed as an overconfident one who's all-in. Diversify because asset classes don't move in tandem (stocks fall, bonds often don't), which lowers volatility without proportionally lowering returns — not diversification as a vague safety blanket. |

## Entrepreneurship / business-building as a wealth path

| Resource-gap read | Framework to reach for | Core mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Wants to start a business as their wealth path and is defaulting to "scale it as big as possible" | Jarvis' Company of One | Growth is optional, not the definition of success — the alternative move is converting rising skill/reputation into a higher rate for flat or fewer hours (more freedom) instead of more headcount and revenue (more stress, more risk, more distance from the work itself). "Staying small on purpose" is a legitimate end state, not a failure to scale; ask which one the person is actually chasing — money, or the freedom money was supposed to buy — before defaulting to "grow it." |
| Already runs/owns a business but feels trapped doing the work instead of building the company | Martell's Buyback Principle (DRIP Matrix) | Calculate a Buyback Rate: (total annual pay from the business) ÷ 8,000. Any task worth less than that per hour belongs in the Delegation Quadrant and should be paid away immediately — treated as an ROI-positive purchase of time, not an indulgence, because the freed hours go back into the Production Quadrant (high money, high energy) where the business actually grows. Harder-to-outsource tasks (Replacement Quadrant: sales, marketing) get systematized and handed off next, not first. |
| Wants to monetize existing expertise — consulting, freelancing, a solo practice — and doesn't know how to charge for it | Weiss' value-based fees (Million Dollar Consulting) | Price the engagement by the value/ROI delivered to the client (rule of thumb: fee ≈ 1/10th of the value created), never by the hour. Hourly billing puts consultant and client in direct conflict — the consultant is paid more for taking longer, which is the opposite of what the client needs. This is the mechanism to hand someone who has the skill but is underpricing it or trading time for money by default. |

## Wealth-path triage: employment vs. investing vs. business

When a wish is genuinely "how do I get rich" with no resource-gap read yet, the fork is usually: (1) has income, needs it *invested* well → the investing-mechanics table above; (2) has no capital to invest and wants leverage beyond a salary → the entrepreneurship table above, picked by whether they're starting from zero (Company of One), already own something that owns them (Buy Back Your Time), or are sitting on unmonetized expertise (Million Dollar Consulting); (3) neither — still building the base habit → back to the original five-framework table. Don't hand someone deep in debt an asset-allocation lecture, and don't hand someone with $2M sitting in cash a debt snowball.

## Known gap

Investing mechanics and entrepreneurship-as-wealth-path are now covered above via real indexed books (Collins, Sethi, Robbins for investing; Jarvis, Martell, Weiss for business-building) — reach for these before improvising. Still genuinely thin here: anything requiring current numbers (specific contribution limits, tax brackets, fund tickers/expense ratios, interest rates) — those change yearly and should come from a live web search, not this file. Also thin: real estate as a wealth vehicle, and business models beyond solo/service businesses (product companies, e-commerce, franchising) — no indexed book covers those yet.
